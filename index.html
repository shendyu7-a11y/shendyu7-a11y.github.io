CASE STUDY · TORQUE TUBE MODIFICATION & FABRICATION PLANNING

Problem. After the Unit 1 & 2 cooling tower retrofit, the new gearboxes kept overheating, raising the risk of an unplanned shutdown. The old gearbox was still sound and could serve as a backup, but its torque tube legs were built for the old structure and did not match the retrofitted beams, so it could not be installed.

Approach.

  • Field survey: measured the old gearbox feet and the new beam mounting points (reverse engineering).
  • 3D modification in SolidWorks: single to double support legs to match the new beam spacing, stiffener plates at critical joints, and a gearbox restraint.
  • Static FEM with design iterations until the safety factor was above 1.
  • Fabrication plan in three phases: incoming material control (BOM, mill test certificates), fabrication and welding per WPS with visual and dye penetrant checks, and final assembly with dimensional and NDT inspection.

Result. Max von Mises stress of 149 MPa against a 170 MPa yield for SS 316L (safety factor 1.14). The design is safe as a short-term mitigation and gives the plant a ready backup for the overheating gearboxes. Because the margin is tight for a dynamic component, a fatigue analysis is recommended before long-term use.
